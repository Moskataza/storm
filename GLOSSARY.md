# User-Directed Information Work

Collection, analysis, processing, and writing of information according to the user's needs across subject areas. Financial research is one example, rather than the system's scope.

## Language

**Information task**:
An assignment to collect, analyze, process, and write information in service of a user's need.

**User need**:
The purpose, questions, or intended outcome that make an information task useful to the user.

**Requester**:
The person whose needs and intended outcome define an information task.

**Operator**:
The person running the system and helping clarify or complete an information task; the requester and operator may be the same person.

**Demand worksheet**:
The editable record of an information task's purpose, questions, scope, value criteria, processing needs, and deliverable requirements, refined through conversation with the user.

**Value criteria**:
Conditions derived from the user's intended outcome that determine whether information and conclusions are useful for a task.

**Guided mode**:
An execution mode in which the user can inspect intermediate findings and redirect the information task.

**Automatic mode**:
An execution mode in which the system carries out a clear information task without routine stage-by-stage user input.

**Data service**:
An external website or API that can supply information for a task, including a source supplied by the requester.

**Service catalogue**:
A searchable collection of optional data services, with descriptions that help a requester choose services relevant to an information task.

**Required service**:
A data service the requester identifies as essential to an information task.

**Task budget**:
The time and cost allowances for planning and completing an information task and its requested deliverables.

**Task plan**:
The scope, work stages, source choices, and allocation of effort designed to complete the requested deliverable within the task budget.

**Finding**:
A task-relevant observation, source claim, reported experience, opinion, or calculated result, accompanied by its supporting evidence and uncertainty.

**Research conclusion**:
An interpretation of the collected information and analysis in relation to the user's needs.

**Visual report**:
A readable presentation of research data and conclusions using charts, tables, and explanatory text, with diagrams or mathematical formulas where useful.

**AI offering**:
A product or packaged capability examined in an AI market study, including hardware, software, agent skills, workflows, prompt packs, agent systems, and platforms. These categories can overlap and expand as the market changes.

**Report bundle**:
The revisioned task result shared by browser, PDF, and future output renderers, containing findings, evidence references, structured datasets, chart specifications, methods, limitations, usage, and output artifacts.

**Task revision**:
An immutable version of a demand worksheet and its resulting plan, evidence, analysis, and deliverables.

**Trusted extension**:
A Python connector, processor, analyzer, or renderer installed by the operator in the local `storm` Conda environment and permitted to run for information tasks.

**Capability probe**:
A dated, bounded test that records what a data service or platform can actually provide through a specified acquisition method.

**Financial fixture**:
A frozen, deterministic, non-advisory set of financial records used to test information workflows without depending on live market data.

**Collection tool**:
An approved connector, web/API client, or browser acquisition capability that an LLM may call to obtain information for an information task.

**Tool-mediated collection**:
LLM-directed information collection performed through approved collection tools with declared capabilities, provenance, and usage limits.
